## Decisions made by Ferdinand

### Working process

- The brand will be built slowly, one small decision at a time.
- Research comes before the visual decision process.
- The questionnaire supplies context. It is not the later decision interface or
  workflow.
- Major decisions remain Ferdinand's. Codex researches, proposes, tests, and
  records only when asked.

### House and faith

- Froni is a distinctly Catholic cultural and fashion house.
- Catholic faith is the reason it exists.
- Religious integrity outranks opportunity.
- Sacred material must not be used chiefly to create demand, altered for trends,
  used without reliable provenance, placed where handling becomes degrading, or
  put on disposable objects.
- Products carrying sacred material remain clothing or objects. Froni does not
  sell blessings or sacramental claims.
- Every work with sacred content receives theological review. The review is
  mandatory and advisory. Ferdinand retains the final judgment.
- Religious explanation should remain brief. Froni may identify the source and
  prevent serious misunderstanding, but should not turn product communication
  into a sermon or a sales interpretation.
- Froni should avoid launching work on feast days.

### Permanent identity and piece-specific identity

- The existing wordmark remains.
- The existing F brandmark is the recurring house signature that may appear
  across products.
- The permanent house identity does not need a new sacred symbol or permanent
  ornament.
- A particular piece may have its own symbol when that genuinely makes sense for
  the piece. It belongs to that piece and does not become a permanent house
  symbol.
- Different works may be radically different when both are exact.
- Recognition cannot depend on forcing every edition to look alike.

### Internal authority

- Ferdinand and his cousin alternate primary creative leadership by piece.
- They may help, challenge, or intervene in each other's work.
- His cousin is the only person who may stop one of Ferdinand's decisions and
  require genuine reconsideration and an attempt at agreement.
- If an impasse remains, Ferdinand has final authority.
- Both outrank decisions made by outside contributors or entities.
- Experts control their domains, but outside contributors do not control the
  work as a whole.

### Work and product range

- One work is public at a time. Later work may be developed privately but stays
  out of public view until the current edition closes.
- Editions remain finite and normally numbered.
- The exact work is not remade.
- An edition is not synonymous with a garment.
- Future editions may include hoodies, coats, full suits, scarves, jewelry,
  rings, eyewear, bags, leather objects, ceremonial clothing, or another form
  appropriate to the work.
- Shoes are presently unlikely, not absolutely prohibited.
- Most works should carry an explicitly sacred subject from a specific
  historical source.
- Every component matters, including hidden and ordinary components. Scrutiny
  reaches material, fiber, pattern, proportion, internal construction, hardware,
  labels, packaging, handling, care, and every other domain relevant to the
  piece.
- Edition Two should represent a major increase in design, detail, material
  specification, component selection, testing, delivery, and care.
- No Edition One feature automatically becomes permanent house law.
- Makers and production remain European. Froni searches for the best maker
  within Europe rather than searching globally and treating Europe as a
  preference.

### Commerce and customer relationship

- No discounts.
- Price is not argued for.
- Froni does not actively persuade someone to purchase.
- Scarcity is factual. Froni does not use hype or manufactured urgency.
- Customer relationships are direct between Froni and each person. Froni does
  not create an owner community.
- A non-buyer may still receive a gift, early access, or another gesture. The
  mechanism is unresolved.
- Future gifts, service, authentication, repair, resale, and later-owner
  relationships require separate decisions.

### Standing commitments

Ferdinand did not establish a public commitment program. He narrowed the field
for later work.

Present candidates include:

- reserving a very small number of works to give;
- allowing a customer to choose a charity in a later edition, probably after
  purchase and with Froni's choice as the default;
- giving without using the gift as marketing;
- sending a useful unannounced gift with purchased work in later editions, with
  Edition One possibly excluded;
- never asking for unpaid speculative creative work;
- credible living-wage conditions;
- continuity with good suppliers over small savings;
- repair, replacement, or refund for failures attributable to Froni;
- authentication and possible take-back;
- never destroying usable unsold work;
- never expanding a published cap; and
- digital accessibility.

Ten percent of profit given away remains a possible mature-house promise, not an
early commitment. No candidate above may be published as a Froni promise without
a separate decision, operating design, legal and financial review where
relevant, and Ferdinand's instruction.

### Voice

- The copywriting document is mostly right, with open edges.
- Froni speaks as an institution and uses Froni as a proper noun.
- The voice may be human, somewhat warm, moderately poetic, moderately
  expansive, and moderately conversational.
- It should create trust, desire, understanding of the making, and confidence in
  practical facts without actively persuading someone to purchase.
- It must not flatter, preach, pressure, test belonging, sell moral identity,
  withhold useful knowledge, or feel corporate.
- Froni does not call itself or its products luxury or premium.
- Numbers should replace vague value claims.
- The word quality may be used in a technical context.
- English remains the main public language. Complete local translations may be
  used where needed.
- Albanian may become a meaningful secondary house language.
- Greek belongs to a particular work rather than holding a permanent house role.

### Visual direction

- Discard the current visual system except for the existing Froni marks.
- Discard the current palette completely.
- No permanent palette constrains the color of a piece.
- A piece may be entirely black, vibrant and richly colored, or anything else
  its design requires.
- The website may change color and atmosphere completely with each edition while
  remaining recognizably Froni.
- Gilda Display and EB Garamond are candidates only. They are not approved as the
  permanent typography.
- The existing page composition and density have no authority.
- The intended first impressions, in order, are Catholic, beautiful, serious,
  alive, exact, unfamiliar, human, and contemporary.
- Froni must not resemble a church gift shop, religious merchandise, a generic
  minimalist high-price template, a museum microsite, a direct-to-consumer
  startup, a generic craft brand, a streetwear release page, an ecclesial
  institution, or a fashion editorial clone.
- Layouts should be object-led and may change with the work.
- A strong grid, frames, large color fields, layering, captions, and marginal
  notes are worth investigating. None has been chosen as a rule.
- Photography should first reveal material and construction. Formal studio
  photography with meaningful human presence is the current starting preference.
- Sacred and historical image cropping remains case by case.
- Motion may be expressive, but speed, readability, usability, and accessibility
  outrank effects.
- Brand, website, and interface references should carry most of the research
  weight.

### Website standard

The website is Ferdinand's greatest branding concern. He described the required
standard as looking like a million dollars.

It must feel:

- exceptionally art-directed;
- substantial;
- fully resolved;
- object-led;
- rich enough to avoid emptiness; and
- controlled enough to avoid clutter.

The main failure modes are:

- thinness;
- visual emptiness;
- excessive information;
- too many competing objects;
- noise;
- generic minimalism;
- decoration without substance; and
- familiar high-price conventions used as a substitute for identity.

The target is substance without clutter. Sparse means controlled and object-led,
not empty.

## Research method

Search recursively. Use early findings to discover related designers, agencies, sectors, awards, interviews, and adjacent sites.

For every candidate:

- Open or fetch the direct website.
- Verify that its principal URL is currently live.
- Record the date checked.
- Remove duplicate domains, redirects, campaign mirrors, and localized versions unless they are materially different.
- Identify the exact pages worth inspecting later, not merely the homepage.
- Prefer direct URLs to the homepage, product page, collection or edition page, archive, editorial page, and any relevant transition state.
- Use design case studies or reputable editorial coverage to support claims about design intent.
- Do not infer a website's current appearance solely from an old article or search thumbnail.
- Do not claim that a site is fast, accessible, or mobile-friendly unless current evidence supports it.
- If a site cannot be rendered, is region-blocked, or relies heavily on JavaScript, mark it for live Chrome verification.
- Distinguish observed facts from your interpretation.
- Do not invent awards, designers, technology stacks, dates, ownership, or performance data.
- Cite factual and analytical claims close to the claim.
- Use direct, unshortened URLs.

Pinterest, Instagram, and directory thumbnails may help discovery, but they are not sufficient evidence for visual analysis.
