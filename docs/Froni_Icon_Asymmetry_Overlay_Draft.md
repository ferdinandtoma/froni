# Froni — Icon Asymmetry Overlay, first pass (draft)

Drafted by Claude, July 14, 2026, from the reference scan (2023 × 3774). Nothing here is final until Ferdinand confirms it in the joint session. The overlay's job: separate the asymmetry that is the painting (reproduce exactly, never correct) from the damage that is the centuries (never reproduce).

Reference: pantocratorStCatherines_2023x3774.jpg. Face midline used for the composites: x = 1028 in full-resolution coordinates. Companion image: Froni_Icon_Mirror_Composites.png — the source face doubled from each side. The two composites read as two different men; a digitizer's sew-out, put through the same test, must differ the same way. If the two sewn composites read as the same face, reject.

Convention: sides are given in viewer terms. Viewer's left is Christ's right (the calm side); viewer's right is Christ's left (the stern side).

## Form — reproduce exactly

- Brows: viewer's left brow arcs higher and rounder, with more air between brow and lid. Viewer's right brow sits lower and flatter, pressing toward the eye.
- Eyes: viewer's left eye opens wider, rounder aperture. Viewer's right eye is narrower, the upper lid heavier. The pupils are not symmetric — the gaze diverges slightly; the catchlights do not match.
- Nose: long, with the axis drifting a touch toward viewer's right at the tip. Nostril shapes differ.
- Mustache: viewer's left sweep is thinner and rises; viewer's right is heavier and drops over the lip corner.
- Mouth: small; viewer's left corner sits slightly raised, viewer's right corner turns down into shadow.
- Cheeks and jaw: viewer's left cheek is fuller and rounder; viewer's right side of the face is narrower and more vertical. This is what the composites show most loudly — the doubled left face is broad and calm, the doubled right face is long and severe.
- Hair: parted off-center; the mass is heavier on viewer's right and comes forward over that shoulder; on viewer's left it recedes behind.

## Surface — do not reproduce

- Craquelure across the entire panel.
- The documented loss zone on Christ's left side — viewer's right: ear, hair, and adjacent face and neck. The mottled, abraded patches in the hair and temple on that side are damage, not modeling.
- Pale flecks and pinpoint losses scattered across forehead, neck, and garment.
- Abrasion in the halo and the punched decoration — the halo is rebuilt clean in marigold per the artwork budgets, not copied wound for wound.
